Spain & the Mediterranean

The first time I traveled to Spain was for a gap year in 2022. I lived in Barcelona for 6 weeks taking immersive Spanish classes, learning about the architecture and culture. It also helped me truly learn what a bullfight was really like.

I didn’t go back to Spain until the Fall of 2025, when I studied abroad in Seville. Before that, I had never seen the south of Spain, so in taking my first ever solo travel trip I walked, trained and ate my way down through Barcelona, Valencia, Alicante, Tabarca, and Buñol for a “small” tomato food fight, and on down to Seville. When I got there I spent 4 months with a host mom and took a semester-long cooking class, learning how to cook over 100 Andalusian Spanish dishes. I also got to see over 10 different cities in southern Spain, immersing myself in one of the richest cultures I have ever seen.

The 4 months I spent in southern Spain taught me more about international markets than I could have imagined, more about expansion practices and international business, and how much culture plays a part in the way we interact. To this day, this is one of my favorite places and one of the most vibrant cultures I have seen.

Thailand & Morocco

Thailand and Morocco are two of my favorite countries in the world. Not only for their beauty in nature, but for their beauty in the people who live there. People who will give you the shirts off their backs when they have nothing, people who constantly have a smile on their faces.

I had the opportunity to spend 6 weeks in Thailand working on conservation projects: learning about mangrove tree and coral reef restoration, tracking monkey migrations, creating garden beds out of eco bricks, farming banana trees and sugar cane, and working to build houses of shade for elephants.

We also worked in summer camps to help children create sustainable mushroom houses they could sell at markets for increased protein in their diet.

In Morocco, on the other side of the world and a completely different place, I was given the opportunity to take a solo trip. I got to see a deeper look into the informal economies that hold this community up, and a beautiful country with so much to offer.

Both of these countries were part of the reason I want to explore more, travel slower and really get to know the place and the people there.
